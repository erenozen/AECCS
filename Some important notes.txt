Unique Contributions
1. PET Effectiveness × Dark Patterns (novel intersection)
No prior study measures whether privacy-enhancing tools actually compensate for dark patterns. You can answer: "If a site uses asymmetric buttons and missing reject, does uBlock Origin neutralize the pre-consent tracking anyway?" Bouhoula 2023 measured dark patterns. Sanchez-Rola 2019 measured PETs. Nobody combined them at scale.

2. Five PETs evaluated simultaneously on the same crawl
Prior PET studies evaluate 1-2 tools. You evaluate uBlock Origin, Privacy Badger, Firefox ETP Standard, Firefox ETP Strict, Brave Shields, and Consent-O-Matic — all against the same 1000 sites, same baseline. This is the most comprehensive cross-PET comparison in published EU GDPR literature.

3. Brave Shields and Firefox ETP as research subjects
These are among the most widely deployed privacy tools (Firefox alone has ~200M users). No GDPR compliance paper has formally benchmarked them against pre-consent tracking at scale.

4. Consent-O-Matic as a PET metric
Consent-O-Matic automates consent rejection. Measuring whether it successfully suppresses tracking (vs. banner-only rejection success) closes a gap — prior work measures CMP compliance but not whether automated consent tools actually reduce data collection.

5. Public sector / government sites in the corpus
Your 1000-site list includes government (.gov.xx) and public-sector domains. Prior studies focus on commercial Alexa/Tranco top sites. Government sites have different legal obligations (stronger, not weaker) and, as your data shows, ~90% non-compliance — a finding no prior paper has documented at scale.

6. 2025/2026 snapshot — post-DMA enforcement era
Nouwens: 2019. Bouhoula: 2021-22. Khandelwal: 2022. Your data is the most current post-DMA (Digital Markets Act, March 2024) snapshot. You can compare pre/post DMA enforcement effects — something no published paper can yet do.

7. Unified end-to-end pipeline (reproducible)
Your open-source pipeline integrates crawling → dark pattern detection → compliance scoring → PET simulation → reporting. Prior studies describe methodology but don't release reproducible pipelines at this scope. This is a methodological contribution.

Race conditions — Trackers fire differently depending on page load timing
Headless detection — Some sites detect and alter behavior for automated browsers inconsistently
This is well-known in web measurement literature — it's measurement variance, not an error.
